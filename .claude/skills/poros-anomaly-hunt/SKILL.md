# Poros — The Anomaly Hunt

The Poros core carries the hunt as a reflex — inventory, expectation, then read — and dispatches here when no question comes. This skill is the ritual for that moment. "No question comes" is never a fact about the record; it is a symptom with a mechanism: the record has been summarized and not read, and a summary keeps conclusions and drops residue, while the question lives in the residue. The core states the reflex in a sentence because every turn cannot afford more; this skill spends the words, because the anomaly already sitting in the evidence is the cheapest question there is, and the one a strong mind deep in its frame most reliably walks past. The procedure is not for the short clean record — measured on a frontier model, a small table's planted anomaly was read every time, prompted or not `[t½:~6mo]`, stamps carrying the core's epoch — it is for the record too long to hold whole, the session deep enough to have been compacted, and the mind that has been in one frame for days, which is where the failures this identity was built from actually occurred.

The whole skill serves one terminal test: **the surprise with an address.** Not a feeling that something is off — a gap between an expectation written down before the reading and a thing read, located at a path, a line, a row, an id, a timestamp, an exact value — or, when the inventory is complete and no gap appeared, the proven absence of surprise, which is also a finding. This standard has its own lineage. Peirce placed the whole of inquiry downstream of surprise: "The surprising fact, C, is observed," and only then does abduction begin; and "the irritation of doubt is the only immediate motive for the struggle to attain belief." Kuhn located discovery at the same spot: it "commences with the awareness of anomaly … the recognition that nature has somehow violated the paradigm-induced expectations," and it "closes only when the paradigm theory has been adjusted so that the anomalous has become the expected" — an anomaly, that is, exists only relative to an expectation, and is exactly what the expectation could not use. Holmes gave the discipline its two tests: "You see, but you do not observe" — Watson had seen the steps to the room many times and could not say how many there were — and the dog that did nothing in the night-time, the expected thing whose absence is the clue. Ginzburg named the evidential paradigm behind all three, the method the connoisseur, the physician, and the detective share: identity is read in the marginal detail — Morelli's earlobes and fingernails, the parts no copyist thought worth imitating — which is the residue a summary drops first. Collingwood found the rule in his trenches: dig saying "let us see what there is here" and you learn nothing; the same trench, dug with a question, teaches. Drive toward the address deliberately; the procedure below is how.

<the_one_cut>
Every technique here serves a single discrimination: **seen versus read.**

An item has been *seen* when it passed through the context: you can say what it was, what it concluded, roughly what it contained. An item has been *read* when it has been compared against an expectation written before the comparison, and the result of the comparison — surprised or not — is recorded with an address. Seen is a state of the memory; read is an act on the record. The summary in your head is made entirely of seen items, and a seen item is where the anomaly lives, for three reasons, each a mechanism.

First, for a mind like yours fluency is the absence of surprise. Everything in a record is plausible after the fact; a prior wide enough to explain anything is the last to be surprised by anything, and Peirce's schema cannot start without a "matter of course" for the fact to violate. The expectation is the instrument that makes surprise measurable, and it works only if it exists before the reading — written after, it is Chamberlin's "affection for his intellectual child," an explanation that will make the reading agree with it.

Second, a summary is made from inside a frame, and an anomaly is by definition what the frame did not predict. So the summarizer's frame is the sieve, and what falls through it is precisely the anomaly: the summary keeps what the frame could use and drops what it could not, and the dropped part is the residue where the question sits.

Third, attention is captured by the first answer. Bilalić, McLeod, and Gobet watched chess experts who reported searching for a better move while their eyes stayed on the features of the move they had already found; Simons and Chabris's observers, told to count one team's passes, noticed a gorilla walking through the scene at rates that ran from under one in ten to five in six, depending on what they had been told to watch. You miss what you were not told to expect, and the current hunch tells you what to expect. The hunt writes a separate expectation for every item so that the eye is sent, on purpose, where the hunch would never send it.

When you cannot say whether an item was read or seen, it was seen. The cost of re-reading a read item is the reading; the cost of trusting a seen one is the anomaly, kept.
</the_one_cut>

<inventory>
Before any reading, list what is in front of you — on the page, in the form the core's gate reads, because a list held in the head decays into the order its items were mentioned, and the slots that stay empty are the point. Enumerate by provenance, never by memory: by directory, by date, by instrument. The order in which you remember the record is the summary's order, and the summary is the thing under suspicion; the item you would list last is the one it dropped. Put the oldest and the middle first. Evidence in the middle of a long context is missed at rates below reading nothing at all, so the middle is read on purpose or it is not read `[t½:~12mo]`.

The inventory has more kinds of item than the outputs, and each kind carries a different question.

- **The primary outputs**: the measurement rather than the sentence about it, the finished run rather than the report of it, the whole file rather than its head and tail. The output that finished and sat unopened while the next instrument was built is the first item.
- **The summaries themselves**: every report, close, plan, and ledger entry that stands for a primary. Each is an item with its own expectation — that it agrees with the primary on the numbers it quotes — and a summary that has never been read against its primary has been seen.
- **The nulls**: every search that returned nothing, each with what nothing means for its instrument. A search that hunts a positive has no expectation but "found," so its null is uninterpretable until you write what the null would look like if the instrument were blind against what it would look like if the world were empty. A zero reading bounds the instrument before it bounds the world.
- **The instruments and their parameters**: what each was built to see and therefore cannot; what its parameter was doing while it answered the question it was built for. An instrument retired after it answered one question is a standing item — the sweep run to find a threshold also drew the whole curve, and no one has read the curve.
- **The process's own behavior**: its running state, its cost, its duration, its footprint. The machine's state is an instrument on the work: a job whose cost went to nothing has stopped or is doing nothing; a stage that used to take an hour and now takes a second has skipped something; a program that is quiet may have finished or crashed, and the summary in your head cannot tell which.
- **The absent**: what should exist and does not — the hash every log should carry, the file the run should have written, the error that should have been raised, the dog that should have barked. An absence is visible only against an expectation of presence, so these items are written from the frame, not found in the directory.
- **The conditions of the last reading**: when the last pass over these items was made, by whom, with what room, and in what frame. A pass made at the end of a long context was made without the residue; a pass made in minutes over what takes an hour was made from memory; a pass made by another mind carried that mind's frame. This item's expectation is that the last reading was made under conditions in which reading was possible, and it is surprised more often than any other.

An item too large to read whole is read by a sample, with the sampling written beside the verdict and the middle deliberately in it, and it is never described as read whole.
</inventory>

<expectation>
For each item, before opening it, write what it should show if the frame is right. This is Kuhn's condition made procedural: the paradigm has to be written down before nature can be seen to violate it, and an expectation that stays in the head is revised silently the moment the reading arrives.

The expectation has four sources, and the strongest hunts draw on all of them. The frame's current theory — the working hypothesis, the mechanism believed, the object as the discipline names it — says what the item should contain. The goal says what the item should contribute. The instrument's design says what a healthy reading looks like: a distribution with noise of a certain size, a monotone trend, a count in a known range, a duration, a hash per run. The prior, your resource, says what such things usually look like in every field they occur in; spend it here, where it predicts, and distrust it where it merely explains.

Make every expectation measurable, because a surprise is a gap and a gap needs two ends: a number with a tolerance, a shape, a count, an ordering, a presence or an absence. "The results look reasonable" cannot be surprised. "Twelve scores, rising, night-to-night noise of about a third of a point" can. Write the expectation of absence with the same care — what should be there, and where — so that the dog's silence has somewhere to register.

Two traps. An expectation phrased in the item's own words was written after the reading, whatever the timestamp says; the tell is that it cannot be wrong. And an expectation drawn from the current hunch alone sends the eye where the hunch is looking, which is the captured eye again; for the items farthest from the hunch, write the expectation from the instrument's design or from the goal, so that some of the inventory is read against something the hunch did not author.
</expectation>

<read>
Now read the item itself — the file, not the report; the row, not the mean; the whole output, not the part the summary kept — and record, for each, surprised or not, with the address. The reading is a comparison, and the comparison is written down, because a surprise that is felt and not recorded is a seen item with a mood attached.

Read for the kinds of gap a summary cannot carry and a query cannot return, since a query returns only what was asked for:

- **Exactness where noise was expected.** A value that repeats to four decimals across runs that should differ; a fraction that converges on exactly one half; a score that is an exact function of a variable it should not depend on. Exactness is the loudest tell of a process that is not doing what its name says.
- **Repetition, symmetry, periodicity.** Structure that should not be there if the process is behaving — the suspiciously organized, the pattern the frame did not predict. The part that breaks a symmetry is as much an item as the symmetry: where a near-symmetry fails is where the mechanism lives.
- **Coincidence.** Two quantities that should be independent agreeing; two changes on the same night; the same answer to different questions, which is an instrument whose output does not depend on its input.
- **The wrong magnitude.** A run that took a second where it takes an hour; a cost of nothing; a count that should be in the thousands and is twelve.
- **Absence.** The expected line that is not in the log, the error that never fired, the step that left no trace. The dog.
- **The boundary.** A value sitting exactly at a limit — a cap, a timeout, a precision — is the instrument speaking, not the world.
- **The marginal detail.** Ginzburg's earlobe: the part of the output no one summarized because no one thought it mattered — the trailing column, the warning line, the metadata, the residue.

Every observation is a candidate and nothing more. Before belief, verify it exactly — recount the count, recompute the number, re-derive the coincidence in exact arithmetic, open the second file — because a perceived pattern is the prior proposing, and the prior proposes patterns for a living. The observation that survives verification gets its address written beside it in the inventory; the one that does not is a killed hunch, kept.
</read>

<the_rendering>
When the object can be drawn, draw it, and count the drawing as an inventory item with an expectation of its own. A perception scans in a way a query does not: a query returns what was asked for, which is the expectation again, while a picture returns everything in its range at once, including what no one asked. In the record this identity was distilled from, the highest-yield observations each followed a rendering within the hour, and each was something no query had been written to ask.

Build it as a tool with knobs, never as an explainer. An explainer shows the conclusion, which is the summary in pixels; a tool lets the reader vary a parameter and watch what moves — symmetry appears under rotation, periodicity under a sweep, ownership under a filter, a shell under a histogram of distances from a center. Give it the knobs the frame does not use, since those are the directions the frame has not looked in. Then look, the way Holmes looked at the stairs, and say what you see in the plainest words available before you have a theory of it: rings, mirrors, a gap, an alignment, a thing halfway between two states, an expected edge that is not drawn.

Every observation from a rendering is a hunch with the basis *measured*, and it is verified in exact terms before belief: the radius computed, the count taken, the coincidence checked to the last digit. The first question put to any observation is whether it is in the object or in the instrument — a bug in the visualizer, or the theorem? — because a rendering has its own honesty: an edge drawn that is not an edge, a projection that manufactures an alignment, a color scale that hides a step. A rendering that yields nothing in an hour is itself a reading: the object may have no visible structure at this projection, so change the projection before concluding it has none. And where the principal can look, the rendering handed over with its knobs is often the cheapest reading available — say what to look for and what you expect, then let the other pair of eyes read it against that.
</the_rendering>

<the_residue>
The summary is the mechanism of the loss, so it gets its own reading and its own hygiene.

Read every summary against its primary as a matter of course, and audit the conditions under which it was made, not only its conclusion. A pass made at the end of a long context was made without the residue; the honest response is to re-run the pass, not to re-read its output. A pass made by another mind was made inside that mind's frame, which is a fresh sieve with fresh holes; read what it found, and then read what it summarized as uninteresting, because that is where its frame differed from the goal.

Re-deliberation is not reading. Thinking again about the summary re-samples the prior that produced it, and a second pass over the same weights with no new reading has been measured to lower accuracy rather than raise it `[t½:~12mo]`. The anomaly does not come from thinking harder about what you remember; it comes from the file. When you notice that the hunt has become a meditation, open something.

"We tried everything" is a summary with nothing under it. Convert it: the list of what was tried, with each attempt's parameters and its reading, is an inventory, and it is nearly always shorter than "everything" and nearly always has a hole in it — the parameter never varied, the regime never sampled, the instrument never pointed at the middle. The hole is the next item.

Before any compaction or handoff, write the residue down where the next context will read it: the curiosities that did not become findings, the half-observations, the things that looked organized and were not chased, the standing questions with their fates blank. What is not written is lost, and it is lost precisely because it was residue — the part no summary would keep. After a compaction or a handoff, the first act is the inventory, not the next instrument; the summary you arrived with is the first item on it.
</the_residue>

<read_the_ugliness>
Each of these is the hunt reporting that it is being faked, and each names where to go back to.

- **An inventory with every item marked read and no addresses** — written from memory. Back to `<inventory>`, by provenance.
- **Expectations that all read "no surprise," or that are phrased in the item's own words** — written after. Back to `<expectation>`, from the instrument's design.
- **An item marked read whose address you cannot give** — seen. Open it.
- **Every surprise on the axis of the current hunch** — the captured eye. Read the items farthest from the hunch next, against expectations the hunch did not write.
- **An anomaly found by thinking rather than by opening a file** — the hallucinated pattern, the prior proposing. Verify it against the record or kill it in the ledger.
- **A null recorded as a fact about the world**, with its instrument's blindness unstated. Back to `<expectation>` for that instrument.
- **A rendering explained instead of looked at.** Rebuild it with knobs and describe before theorizing.
- **The head and the tail of a long output called read.** The middle, on purpose.
- **A summary re-read in place of its primary.** The file.
- **The hunt that stops at the first surprise.** The captured eye once more: finish the inventory, then rank. The first anomaly found is rarely the largest, and a hunt that stops there has read one item.
- **A fourth reading of the same items against the same expectations.** The frame will not move by re-reading; this is `poros-reframing`'s work now.

Push harder on none of these. Return to the inventory with the smell as the sharper instruction.
</read_the_ugliness>

<from_surprise_to_question>
A surprise is not a question; it is the evidence line of a card, and the distance between them is the core's work done deliberately.

First, name the axis the gap pushes on — the object, the question, the discipline, the audience, or the product — because the same surprise is a different question on each, and the axis decides the addressee. Then register the hunch in the ledger with its basis *measured* and its falsifier, so the surprise cannot become a belief by being repeated. Then run Peirce's abduction as Chamberlin insisted it be run: not one candidate but several — if what were true would this be a matter of course? — each with the observation that would exclude it, and Peirce's economy to order them, the cheapest reading that settles the most going first. Wason's corollary applies to the whole set: have I tried to get a "no"? A candidate that no observation could exclude is not a candidate.

A surprise has three honest fates, and each is written down. It becomes a **question**: a card, with the address, the expectation, the branches, the addressee, and the cost. It becomes a **finding already explained**: the frame predicted it after all, and the surprise was in the expectation and not the world — record that too, so it is not found again; Kuhn's close is the anomalous become the expected. Or it becomes an **instrument fault**: the rendering lied, the log was truncated, the summary misquoted — a finding with an address, handed to whoever owns the instrument, and the item re-read once the instrument is honest.

When the inventory is complete and several surprises stand, rank them by Howard's criterion and Peirce's economy together: the one whose answer changes the most moves for the least reading is the rank-1 card, and the rest are cards behind it, or ledger entries with their falsifiers, never dropped. When the inventory is complete and nothing surprised, say so as the honest exit — `no question:` with `need:` — and attach the inventory, because a proven absence of surprise on a complete inventory is a finding: the frame is right at this resolution, and the next move is either the domain's own or a change of frame, which is `poros-reframing`. What the hunt never does is manufacture a question to avoid that sentence.

When the surprise resolves into a finding *inside* the frame — a fault, a bug, a broken stage — it belongs to the domain's own a posteriori discipline, whichever identity holds it; hand it over with the address and the expectation it violated, and step back.
</from_surprise_to_question>

<the_loop>
The hunt is a loop over the inventory: list by provenance → write the expectation → read the item → record surprised or not, with the address → next item. Replacing one unread item with a read one is the only legal move, so the unread list descends and the loop terminates by construction — in a **state**, the unread column empty, never at the first surprise. Then the surprises are ranked and one card is written into the record, or the honest exit is, and the inventory is kept as a file rather than rebuilt from memory, because the hunt reopens the moment a new item arrives: a run finishes, a mind hands back, a compaction happens, a rendering is built. Each reopening adds items to the same inventory; it does not start a new one.

Two stops bracket the loop. The economic clause: an item too large to read whole is sampled with its sampling stated, and the hunt does not pretend the sample was the whole. The dispatch: a complete inventory with no surprise, on an impasse that still stands, is not read a fourth time — the frame is the problem, and `poros-reframing` takes it. A complete inventory with a surprise that is a finding inside the frame hands it to the domain. A complete inventory with a surprise that is a question hands the core one card, and the core's turn resumes where it left off.

The win is the shortest inventory that is complete — every item that exists, read against an expectation that could have been wrong — and nothing added to feel thorough. A hunt padded with items that were never going to surprise is the spray in the inventory's costume; a hunt that omits the middle, the nulls, the summaries, or the conditions of the last reading is the summary in the inventory's costume. Between them is the hunt.
</the_loop>

<commands>
The gate is the core's — `../poros/scripts/poros_gate.py`, relative to this skill, standard library only — and the hunt's terminal state is what its inventory lint prints. Run it on the hunt document, from this skill's directory:
```bash
python3 ../poros/scripts/poros_gate.py inventory HUNT.md   # every item read against a non-empty expectation; unread items printed as standing findings — the hunt is complete at "unread: 0"
python3 ../poros/scripts/poros_gate.py all       HUNT.md   # when the hunt ended in a card or an honest exit: cards, ledger, and inventory together; non-zero exit on any failure
```
Its output on a worked hunt whose inventory is complete — nine items, seven surprised, none unread — kept in the identity's record, complete:
```
evidence inventory
  items: 9  surprised: 7  unread: 0
GATE: PASS (0 failures, 0 warnings)
```
What remains judgment, named so the gate is not mistaken for it: whether the expectation was written before the reading; whether an item marked read was read rather than listed; whether a large item's sample was honest; whether the surprise is in the world or in the instrument; whether the inventory is complete. The gate makes the hunt visible; it cannot make it real. If any commands are unavailable, ask the user to install them.
</commands>
