# de Bruijn — Obligation Modeling

The core carries the per-turn reflexes: the green manifold, the descending decomposition, automation cited at leaves. This skill is the ritual for when an obligation resists — a goal surviving its third tactic, an induction refusing its step, a termination argument you cannot name — the slow explicit descent `debruijn-domain-modeling` performs for representations, transposed into proof space. The two are one doctrine on two faces — **types are the image of the data's structure; statements are the image of the argument's structure** — and `debruijn-experience-modeling` is its third, where the surface is the image of the user's situation.

In the Rust lineage this skill supplied a discipline the compiler could not check; Lean mechanizes the checking and keeps the finding, which is the harder half. Curry–Howard stops being an analogy here: the recursion is the induction, the invariant is the statement, the measure is the `termination_by`, and the kernel will verify any of them while locating none. The terminal test, the analogue of the beautiful model: **the statement from which the proof derives.** When the statement is right, the induction is the recursor the type already generates, each case closes in a line or two, and the proof reads as a transcription of the argument. While a goal still needs a walkthrough, the statement is wrong or too weak. The lineage is the calculational tradition of Dijkstra, Gries, and Back — program and proof developed as a single act, every step forced, nothing ingenious — joined by Polya's inventor's paradox: the more general statement is often the easier one to prove. Drive toward it deliberately; the procedure below is how.

<the_one_cut>
Generation is a total order — one token, one step, one narrative thread — and untrained reasoning inherits the stride: it believes a claim by running an example. The objects here are spaces: every input, every case, every branch of the induction. An `#eval`, a worked example, a trace through one member is evidence incidental — true of this instance — where the claim required is intrinsic, quantified over every member the type admits. **An example can falsify, never verify** — Dijkstra's aphorism, and in Lean it acquires teeth: the space-level claim is not merely desirable but *submittable*, so the gap between "checked on three inputs" and "proven" is the gap between `#guard` and `theorem`, visible in the file.

The resolution — the master move the whole skill serves — is the **local obligation**: a finite, per-case check whose discharge implies the global claim. An induction hypothesis every case preserves. A measure every recursive call strictly decreases. An invariant every loop iteration re-establishes. A congruence every rewrite respects. One idea in many costumes, and in Lean each costume is a goal the kernel will actually check: the global claim is unenumerable, the local obligation is a two-line case, and the recursor bridges them. A claim with no local obligation behind it is a `sorry` wearing prose.

The token stream and the goal state are your work tape — a chain of reasoning longer than one glance exists only where it is externalized, and the InfoView externalizes for free. An elided step ("the other cases are similar") is a step guessed, never computed; make the machine take it.
</the_one_cut>

<characterize_the_space>
Before tactics, produce the space as an artifact — and the space of a proof is generated by its induction, so characterizing it means choosing the recursion that carves it. The options, cheapest adequate first: **structural** induction — the recursor the type already owns, free if `debruijn-domain-modeling` chose the type backward from this proof; **functional** induction — `fun_induction f`, the induction that mirrors `f`'s own recursion, so the proof's cases are the definition's cases and the awkward mismatch between them vanishes `[t½:~6mo]`; **well-founded** induction on a named measure — `termination_by` at the definition, strong induction at the proof; a **custom eliminator** when the domain's natural case split is none of these. The induction chosen is the space characterized; a proof fighting its cases is almost always a proof running the wrong recursion.

Write the case table before the first tactic: the skeleton `induction xs with | nil => sorry | cons x xs ih => sorry` *is* the space written out — every node named, every given and owed visible in the goal state, nothing elided. The machine holds full state at every node of the tree; use it instead of narrative.

Characterize the obligations themselves while you are here: partition every claim the milestone makes into **prove** (the properties the design leans on), **test** (concrete facts, `#guard`, spec sanity — the instruments that catch a wrong statement), and **assume** (named hypotheses the caller must discharge). The partition is design, made deliberately once, rather than emerging from whatever automation happened to close.
</characterize_the_space>

<derive_the_statement>
The statement is the image of the space's intrinsic structure, and finding it is the same act as writing the proof. Derive them together: attempt the induction with `sorry` at every case, then read each stuck case as a *report* — the goal it shows is precisely what the statement fails to carry through the step. The derivation dial has two directions, and stuck proofs name which to turn:

**Strengthen** when the hypothesis cannot carry itself: the characteristic wrong turn is patching the tactic instead. The canonical case, with the move reified as syntax:
```lean
-- Stuck: proving  revAux xs [] = xs.reverse  — the IH speaks only of [].
-- Strengthen: quantify the accumulator; every case now closes.
theorem revAux_eq (xs acc : List α) : revAux xs acc = xs.reverse ++ acc := by
  induction xs generalizing acc with
  | nil => simp [revAux]
  | cons x xs ih => simp [revAux, ih]
```
`generalizing` is Gries's fresh variable, `induction` is his loop, and the tupled accumulator (maximum-segment-sum's four-tuple) is the same move when one quantity cannot compose alone — widen the induction hypothesis until each case closes locally.

**Weaken** to manufacture the first candidate — Polya's easier problem, made mechanical: replace a constant with a variable, delete a conjunct and re-establish it at exit, enlarge a range until the base case is vacuous. The auxiliary lemma is this move named: the easier claim proven separately, the original recovered as its instance at the boundary.

Name the measure for every recursion, and name it yourself — "it obviously finishes" is what `failed to prove termination` rejects, and the checker demands exactly what this section derives: `termination_by` the measure, `decreasing_by` the strict decrease, with the decrease argument two lines because the measure was chosen to make it so. For monadic and imperative-shaped code, the Hoare discipline arrives natively: `Std.Do`'s `mvcgen` generates the verification conditions and loop invariants are annotations — Gries's method as an API; young, verify at point of use `[t½:~3mo]`.

Back's structural corollary survives translation intact: shape the *definition* around the statement, keeping the statement simple, even when convention argues for a different shape — include the zero case that kills a side condition, define on `≤` when the theorems want `≤`, add the redundant field the proofs keep reconstructing. The proof is the load-bearing artifact; the definition is its readout. When that reshaping grows structural, it is `debruijn-domain-modeling`'s recursor-backward move — hand it over.
</derive_the_statement>

<meta_shapes>
A handful of master shapes reorganizes the catalog — and in Lean they are citable objects, so recognition ends in an import rather than a description:

- **Monotone map on a complete lattice** → least fixed point exists (Knaster–Tarski, `OrderHom.lfp`), and a worklist reaches it under *any* processing order; ordering affects only speed. Dataflow, constraint propagation, unification wear it. Newman's lemma is the kin license for rewriting — local confluence plus termination buys global confluence — and a simp set is exactly such a rewrite system, which is why the core's global_speech_acts legislates it.
- **Optimal substructure over a subproblem DAG** — the DP entry's declared meaning *is* the statement, written before any recurrence; a recurrence inexpressible in the entry you hold is the strengthening cue, the four-tuple generalized. A cycle among subproblems demotes this to the lattice shape above.
- **Associative combine** → every evaluation tree agrees → fold/scan parallelism. In Lean the demand sits in the signature — `Finset.sum` requires `AddCommMonoid`, the foldl/foldr agreement lemmas name their commutativity hypotheses — so the perjury Rust could not detect is a type error here, *provided your own combinators demand what they use*. The third homomorphism theorem remains the existence test: computable as both a leftward and rightward fold means the associative combine exists, though extracting it may cost the strengthening move.
- **Galois connection** — `GaloisConnection`, the shape of every abstraction, adjunction, and bound-transfer argument; when two orders trade a "best over-approximation," search for the connection before proving its consequences by hand — `gc.le_iff_le` and kin are the two-line closes.
- **Measure and potential, one object** — a function on states whose per-step delta is local and whose telescoped sum is the global claim: strictly negative proves termination (`termination_by`, named), bounded-by-payment proves amortized cost — one obligation, two currencies. **Exchange arguments** are the greedy sibling: each adversarial swap non-worsening, induction carrying local to global.
- **Reduction to a named theorem** — the strongest move in the language: your claim as an instance of something Mathlib already proved. Loogle the shape before deriving anything (core: documentation_first); a morning of derivation is a failed search. Its automation face: recognize the goal's home fragment and route it — linear integer arithmetic → `omega`; closed decidable → `decide`; bitvectors → `bv_decide`; equational/congruence case bash → `grind`, `simp` — and hand-prove exactly what falls in no fragment `[t½:~6mo]`. Its stop signal: a goal that is genuinely undecidable or classically hard does not yield to a longer tactic block; change instruments.

The catalog's discipline: every shape is a theorem with hypotheses, and in Lean the checker holds you to them — but only if your statements carry them. A lemma stated without the hypothesis it silently uses is perjury deferred to the seam.
</meta_shapes>

<transcribe_declaratively>
The proof construct is a claim about the space; choose the one whose claim is your argument.

- **`induction … with` named cases** is the partition proof, every node labeled; an unlabeled tactic cascade over the same goals is the identical tree with the labels sanded off — illegible to the reader and to future automation alike.
- **`calc`** is the equational argument with every step cited — the calculational tradition's native syntax; a chain of `rw`s computes the same rewrite while smearing the argument across the transcript.
- A **`have` chain** is induction's narrative form: each stepping stone typed, so the failure localizes to the joint that owns it (core: style_structured).
- A **fold with a stated lemma API** is induction reified in program space: the accumulator's type is the invariant, and `foldl_eq`, `foldl_append` are the preservation steps, proven once.
- **`decide`** on a finite claim is a faithful transcription — the argument *is* "check them all," and the kernel checks them all; forcing a calc chain onto it imposes incidental style, exactly as forcing tactic soup onto an equational argument does in reverse.

The witnessing hierarchy, in preference order: the obligation the **kernel** discharges (the theorem itself); else reified as a **value** (a `Decidable` instance, a `Subtype`, a bundled structure — placement per the sibling's cut); else checked at **elaboration** on concrete members (`#guard`, the spec examples); else asserted at **runtime** in dev builds. Every rung carries its proof; the rungs differ only in the witness — and each descent is a trust purchase the core's inventory records.

Declarative means structure-transcribing, never tactic minimalism or maximalism: a proof honestly a case bash should be `grind` or `decide` at the leaf and say so; a proof honestly an equational derivation should be `calc` and cite each step; forty lines of interleaved `rw`/`simp` that "works" is a walkthrough in costume — it closes the goal and transcribes nothing.
</transcribe_declaratively>

<read_the_ugliness>
Each smell is the space reporting a mischaracterization; read it as information:

- **The induction hypothesis goes unused** → inducting on the wrong variable or the wrong relation — find the recursion the definitions actually perform (`fun_induction`), or admit the proof wanted `cases`.
- **A case whose informal argument is obvious refuses to close** → the statement is too weak to carry itself through the step; strengthen — `generalizing`, the tupled conclusion, the auxiliary quantity — and the patch you were about to make to the tactic block is the wrong branch.
- **The same `simp only [...]` chain repeated in every case** → a missing lemma or a wrong simp-normal form; mint the lemma once — the sibling's role-naming move, in lemma space.
- **A peeled base case, an induction starting at 1, an off-by-one at the anchor** → the statement is one element off from the space; re-anchor so initialization is vacuous — include the empty case, start at zero, shift the variable.
- **Casts and `Eq.mpr` accumulating inside the proof** → representation smell surfacing dynamically; the entanglement is the type's, and `debruijn-domain-modeling` owns it.
- **A hypothesis the proof never touches** → dead weight to delete, or a statement accidentally provable without what you believed essential — check vacuity at the seam before celebrating the generality.
- **A forty-line tactic block that closes the goal** → the obligation was never localized; decompose into `have` children with stated types, or concede the case bash to `grind` explicitly.
- **The `sorry` count stable across turns** → the decomposition is not descending; you are rephrasing one goal in a circle, and the loop's own measure has caught you — strengthen the statement or dispatch out.

Push harder on none of these. Return to `characterize_the_space` or `derive_the_statement` with the smell as the sharper question.
</read_the_ugliness>

<validate_adversarially>
The epistemology enforces at the exit, in rank order: an example falsifies; `plausible` falsifies wider; the kernel verifies — *the statement you wrote*, which is why validation here aims past the checkmark at the statement itself.

**Decorrelate the check from the generator.** Re-reading a proof you just authored re-samples the prior that produced it, and a green checkmark makes the re-read feel unnecessary — the failure mode is not a wrong proof but a proven wrong. Independence is procedural: state the theorem's meaning in words *before* re-reading its formalization and compare; run the mutation audit — perturb the statement (drop a hypothesis, swap `≤` for `<`, specialize a variable) and confirm provability *breaks*, because a statement whose weakenings all still prove was vacuous or over-hedged; delete each hypothesis once and let the proof's failure certify it load-bearing. `#print axioms` on every public theorem — a `sorry` three imports deep inherits silently. Keep the sibling's adversarial member as a negative test: `example : ¬ Bad …` pins the intent the positive statements cannot.

Concurrency's residue is thin and stays thin: pure code is race-free by construction, so the conflict-pair audit applies only at the rim — enumerate the `IO.Ref`/`Std.Mutex` sites, attach to each the invariant that holds whenever the lock is free, and note that a `Task` boundary is a rely/guarantee pair stated in one line each. The discipline survives; the acreage it governed does not.

When an obligation fails twice, re-derive from the characterization in a fresh frame rather than patching a third time: the committed attempt is now a high-weight exemplar anchoring every subsequent attempt toward itself — and the `?`-variants give the fresh frame mechanically, since `exact?` and `simp?` sample the library's prior instead of yours.
</validate_adversarially>

<the_loop>
Characterize → derive → transcribe → read the ugliness → validate — an oscillation between altitudes: descend into one obligation under narrow focus, and when a smell or a stuck case speaks, ascend to the statement with a sharper question rather than grinding at the goal. Each pass strips an assumed anchoring, a dead hypothesis, an induction that was never the space's own. You are done when the statement, the induction, and the proof are one object seen three ways — the proof is the recursor applied to the statement, each case two lines, and the next pass changes nothing.

The descent obeys the core's loop: every turn replaces one `sorry` with finitely many strictly smaller ones under a measure you could state aloud, so the burn-down terminates by construction — and a turn that cannot name its measure is the flailing this skill exists to interrupt. When a discharged obligation later disagrees with a running program, or a Mathlib bump breaks a proof that was allegedly robust, the obligation was discharged against a model of the environment rather than the environment — `debruijn-debugging`'s inverse pass recovers which assumption lied; the answer is an observation, never another derivation. Stop there. The win is the least statement that carries its own induction, and one quantifier more is incidental structure creeping back in.
</the_loop>
